Home wifi & network

Label your router and modem

If you have two boxes for your internet, put a sticker with a "1" on one and a "2" on the other. Then if you ever call for help, you can just say "the light on box 2 has turned red" instead of trying to describe which box is which.

Give your wifi just one name

Some homes have two separate wifi signals hiding under different names. You don't need to know why — just ask us to combine them into one name, so your phone or tablet always picks the strongest signal by itself.

Put smart gadgets on their own wifi

Many routers let you create a second "guest" wifi network. It's a good idea to put things like smart plugs and doorbells on this separate network, keeping them apart from your computer and your important accounts.

Smart TVs & streaming

One simple remote instead of three

If you're juggling separate remotes for the TV, the box and the sound system, ask about a single "universal" remote with large, clear buttons that does the lot.

Make the talking clearer

If music or background noise drowns out what people are saying, look for "Night Mode" or "Dialogue Boost" in your TV's sound settings. It brings the voices forward so it's easier to follow along.

Move your favourites to the front

Ask us to move your 3 or 4 most-used apps — like iPlayer or Netflix — right to the front of your TV's home screen, and tuck the ones you never use out of the way.

Smart speakers & voice control

Teach it one "good morning"

You can set up a smart speaker, like Alexa or Google Home, so that saying just "good morning" makes it read out the weather, the news headlines and what's in your diary — all in one go.

Give yourself longer to speak

If your smart speaker keeps cutting you off before you've finished your sentence, there's a setting that gives you a little more time to speak before it responds.

Talk to another room without shouting

Many smart speakers let you speak to a speaker in another room, a bit like an intercom. Handy for saying "dinner's ready" or checking in with someone upstairs.

Video doorbells & home security

See who's at the door from the kitchen

Your video doorbell can send its picture to a smart screen in the kitchen, so you can see who's outside without getting up or going to the door.

Stop it buzzing at every passing car

Doorbell cameras often send an alert for every car or dog walker going past. We can narrow the area it watches, so it only lets you know when someone's actually at your door.

Let family keep an eye on things too

You can give a son, daughter or trusted neighbour access to your doorbell camera, so more than one person can check in if something doesn't look right.

Online safety & fraud prevention

Stop confusing pop-ups

A trusted "ad-blocker" — a simple bit of free software — can stop a lot of the flashing pop-up windows and adverts that make browsing the internet stressful and confusing.

Send scam callers straight to voicemail

Your phone has a setting called something like "Silence Unknown Callers". Turn it on, and calls from numbers you don't know go straight to voicemail instead of ringing through.

Decide who looks after things later

Apple and Google both let you name a trusted person — called a "Legacy Contact" — who can access your photos and accounts if something happens to you. A kind, practical thing to set up in advance.

Easy screen & phone settings

Make the writing bigger and bolder

Every phone has a setting to make all the writing on the screen bigger and easier to read — not just in one app, but everywhere. We can turn this on for you in a couple of minutes.

Turn off the fiddly finger-swipes

Some phones expect complicated swipes with two or three fingers to do simple things. You can turn this off, so a normal, single tap does the job instead.

Let paramedics see your key details

You can set up a simple health profile that shows on your lock screen, so in an emergency, a paramedic can see things like your allergies or medication without needing to unlock your phone.

A note for the family

If you're helping a parent or relative, this bit's for you

You want to help, and that's lovely — but a few small habits make all the difference between help that builds confidence and help that quietly undermines it.

Let them hold the phone

It's tempting to just take the phone and fix it yourself. But watching someone else do it doesn't build confidence — doing it themselves, even slowly, is what actually sticks.

Agree on one point of contact

If several family members all give different advice, it gets confusing fast. Agree who handles the calls to me, so nobody's getting mixed messages about what was changed and why.

Ask before you change anything

If you spot something on a visit home that looks "wrong", ask first rather than quietly changing it. It's still their phone, and unexplained changes can feel unsettling rather than helpful.

Loop me in for anything you're not sure about

If you're ever unsure whether a message, call or "urgent" pop-up is a scam, you're welcome to call or message me directly for a second opinion — for free, no obligation.

Set up the safety nets early

Things like doorbell camera sharing, Legacy Contacts, and spam call filtering are far easier to set up calmly in advance than to sort out during a stressful moment later on.

Consider a regular check-in

If you live far away and worry about staying on top of things, the Peace of Mind Membership means someone is calling to check in regularly, even when you can't be there yourself.
