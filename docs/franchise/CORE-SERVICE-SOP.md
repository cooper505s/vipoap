# Core service procedure

## 1. Enquiry and booking

Search before creating a customer to avoid duplicates. Confirm name, service address, contact preference, problem, accessibility needs and whether someone else will attend. Explain the call-out price, included time/work, likely exclusions, cancellation terms and aftercare. Record the source and assign the correct territory.

## 2. Before travel

Review the customer, equipment, previous calls and flagged follow-ups. Confirm the appointment and take only the equipment needed. Do not export customer data to a personal device or unapproved app.

## 3. Arrival and consent

Wear approved identification, introduce yourself and confirm the agreed scope before starting. Ask permission before moving items, photographing equipment, accessing accounts or changing settings. Explain any material risk or extra cost and record approval.

## 4. Complete the work

Work carefully, preserve existing settings where practical and make one controlled change at a time. Do not make purchases, accept gifts beyond policy, conduct banking, or retain passwords. Stop if the request is unsafe, outside competence or may facilitate fraud.

## 5. Record the visit

Before closing the call-out, record arrival/departure or time spent, work performed, outcome, equipment and identifiers where appropriate, files/photos with consent, costs, membership use, customer notes and possible upgrades. Recommendations are advisory, not a pressure sale.

## 6. Handover and payment

Show the customer the result in plain language. Explain anything unfinished, security actions and next steps. Confirm the final amount against the agreed basis, issue the invoice/receipt and record payment status.

## 7. Aftercare

Send or provide a concise completion summary. Schedule the promised follow-up—normally sooner for unresolved or higher-risk work—and record its outcome. Reopen or escalate problems rather than creating disconnected notes.

## 8. Escalation

Escalate immediately: suspected fraud or abuse, safety concerns, a data incident, a lost device, serious complaint, disputed consent or payment, repeat failure, work outside authority, or any situation where the customer may be at risk. Preserve factual records and do not investigate sensitive concerns alone.
