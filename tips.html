Advice & guides

Simple answers to common technology questions

Short, practical guidance written in plain English.

Something feels suspicious?

Stop, do not click, and call someone you trust. I can help you check it.

Safety A caller says your computer has a problem

Hang up. Genuine companies do not normally call unexpectedly and ask to take control of your computer. Never share a password or banking code.

Wi-Fi Your internet is slow in one room

Try the same device near the router. If it improves, the issue is probably Wi-Fi coverage rather than your broadband line.

Phones Choosing a new phone

Think about screen size, battery life and how long updates will be available. You do not need the most expensive model for calls, photos and messages.

Passwords Make passwords easier and safer

Use a few unrelated words that you can remember, and use a different password for important accounts. A password manager can help.

Messages A text asks you to pay quickly

Do not use the link in the message. Open the company’s official app or type its known website address yourself.

Buying Before buying new equipment

Write down what you want it to do. Independent advice can stop you paying for features you will never use.

It is always fine to ask

There is no such thing as a silly technology question.
